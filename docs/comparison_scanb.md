# Selector comparison on SCAN-B

NEXT.md steps 2 and 6. Run 2026-10-31 with `configs/comparison_scanb.yaml`:
seven methods, k = 5 to 1000, logistic regression with C tuned inside each
split, the five PAM50 subtype-vs-rest tasks, 16 splits at train fraction
0.5. Random and Mann-Whitney also run at k = 2855, to compare HC2008 at its
own size. 3,520 executions in 29 minutes on 4 workers, data in float32, 50
trees in the forest. Protocol: `docs/report/04_methodology.md`.

Tables generated by:

    python scripts/summarize_comparison.py results/comparison_scanb.csv \
        --n-features 28552 --out <file>

Each cell is the median over the 16 splits, with the first and third
quartiles in brackets.

## What it shows

- The gain over random selection is largest at k = 5: 0.26 to 0.29
  macro-F1 for Mann-Whitney, Lasso and tree importance. It shrinks with
  every step of k: 0.02 to 0.05 at k = 250, and at most 0.025 at k = 1000.
- Tree importance has the highest median at every k up to 250. Its
  quartiles lie wholly above Mann-Whitney's and Lasso's at k = 5, 50, 100
  and 250. At k = 500 and 1000, Mann-Whitney catches up with it.
- Lasso stops improving at k = 20, near 0.845. It gives a non-zero weight to
  about 330 genes only, so at k = 500 and 1000 it keeps fewer genes than
  asked and falls behind random selection at k = 1000.
- RFE is the weakest selector. Its gain is clearly above zero only up to
  k = 20. From k = 50 its quartiles include zero, and at k = 500 and 1000
  they lie below it. It fails on LumA,
  LumB and Normal at small k.
- HC2008 keeps 2,855 genes, the edge of its search, on every task and split,
  and equals Mann-Whitney at that size. It gains 0.017 [0.014, 0.018] over
  random selection of as many. HC2004 keeps 1 gene on most tasks and scores 0.638.
- Mann-Whitney and HC2008 are the most stable (Kuncheva 0.67 to 0.87). Tree
  importance is the least (0.08 to 0.19), Lasso and RFE lie between (0.20
  to 0.30).

## Caveats

- The quartiles are over re-splits of the same patients, not a confidence
  interval.
- RFE uses step 0.5, half the genes per round, chosen for speed.
- The forest has 50 trees, chosen for speed. Fewer trees make its gene
  choices less stable; on BreastMNIST 200 trees gave about the same scores.
- Selection times are per fit on one of 4 workers that share the machine.
