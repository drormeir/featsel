# Selector comparison on SCAN-B: first complete run

NEXT.md step 2. Run 2026-10-31 with `configs/comparison_scanb.yaml`: seven
methods, k = 10, 50 and 250, logistic regression with C tuned inside each
split, the five PAM50 subtype-vs-rest tasks, 5 splits at train fraction 0.5.
425 executions. Protocol: `docs/report/04_methodology.md`.

Tables generated by:

    python scripts/summarize_comparison.py results/comparison_scanb.csv \
        --n-features 28552 --out <file>

Each cell is the median over the 5 splits, with the range in brackets.

## What it shows

- At k = 10, Mann-Whitney, tree importance and Lasso beat the random
  control by 0.25 to 0.28 macro-F1. At k = 250 the gain shrinks to 0.02 to
  0.05: 250 random genes already carry most of the signal.
- RFE is the weakest selector: 0.12 over random at k = 10, none at k = 250.
  At k = 10 it fails on LumA (0.46) and Normal (0.51).
- HC2008 scores best overall, 0.884. It keeps 2,855 genes on every task,
  which is the edge of its search (alpha0 = 0.1 of 28,552). That limit, not
  the data, sets its count.
- HC2004 keeps 1 gene and scores 0.637.
- Mann-Whitney and HC2008 are the most stable (Kuncheva 0.68 to 0.87).
  Lasso, tree importance and RFE choose different genes on each split (0.19
  to 0.43).

## Caveats

- Five splits give a rough spread. It is not a confidence interval.
- RFE uses step 0.5, two rounds, chosen for speed. Step 0.1 gave about the
  same scores in an earlier run of this config (0.686 against 0.676 mean
  macro-F1 at k = 10).
- Lasso keeps only genes with a non-zero weight. For Basal at k = 250 that is
  as few as 155 genes.
- The rows were computed in several passes with 6, 3 and 2 workers, after an
  out-of-memory stop and two fixes (RFE and Lasso). Selection times are
  therefore not comparable across methods. The selections and scores do not
  depend on the number of workers.
