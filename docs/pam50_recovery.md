# PAM50 gene recovery on SCAN-B

Step 6 of NEXT.md, designed in `docs/report/03_data.md`. How deep in each
method's ranking do the 50 PAM50 genes lie? The labels are computed from
these 50 genes, so the answer is known. Gene list:
`featsel_datasets/scanb_gse96058/pam50.py`.

## What it shows

- Every method finds PAM50 genes far faster than chance. Mann-Whitney is
  best: half of the 50 lie in its top 1,041 genes (median), where random
  selection needs about 14,300.
- Tree importance is second at small k, and equal to Mann-Whitney up to
  k = 1000, but its last genes lie deep (90% only by rank 22,848).
- RFE recovers few: 3% at k = 50. Its scores were also the weakest.
- Lasso stops at 16%: it gives a non-zero weight to too few genes to reach
  the rest.
- Recall at k = 50 is low for every method, at most 26% (Mann-Whitney on
  LumA). Each task needs only part of the 50, and each PAM50 gene moves
  with hundreds of others that predict as well. Low recall here does not
  mean poor prediction (`docs/report/03_data.md`).
- The 10-split numbers differ from the earlier 2-split run by at most 0.03.

## Limits

- 10 splits (the first run used 2).
- Recall counts all 50 genes in every task, though a single subtype may
  depend on only some of them.
